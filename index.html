CURRENT RULES

Grammar notes

A compact guide based on Numa Grammar & Dictionary v1.0.0.

Word order

The default order is Subject–Verb–Object (SVO). Ordinary sentences use mostly fixed word order.

Pali penu mako.The person sees the house. (roots provisional)
mi- depends on what it attaches to

Before a noun, mi- means “my.” Before a verb, it marks the subject “I.”

mi-makomy house / my home
mi-yavi Numa.I speak Numa.

mi- is not a standalone pronoun. Misa means “I/me.”

Possession with ti-

ti- attached to a noun means “your / belonging to the listener.” A second-person verb prefix has not been approved.

ti-makoyour house / your home
Negation with no-

Attach no- to a verb to negate the action. The standalone response non means “no.”

Pali no-penu mako.The person does not see the house.
Questions

Speech can mark questions through intonation. In writing, use an ordinary question mark. Numa does not have a finalized set of dedicated question words; a category or topic can be placed first when needed.

Veta mako?Are you going home? Context supplies “you.”
Movement and destinations

A destination can follow a movement verb directly. A special “to” word or prefix is not required.

Misa veta mi-mako.I am going to my house.
One adjective suffix per word

Only one adjective suffix may attach to a word under the current chosen direction. These suffix meanings are provisional.

mako-kismall house
mako-rolarge house

mako-ki-ja is invalid under this current rule.

Writing system status

Numa's writing system is intended to be meaning-based/logographic, with organic simplified drawing-like characters. Compounds are expected to have their own learned pronunciations and writing direction is left-to-right. The actual glyph inventory and compound-writing rules are still unfinished.
